— What AI changed, and what it didn’t

You can build almost anything with AI now. You shouldn’t trust all of it.

WHAT CHANGED

Anyone can build a tool fast

A follow-up bot or a lead router can come together in an afternoon.

WHAT DIDN’T

Untested tools fail quietly

A follow-up stops sending. A lead never reaches the CRM. Nobody notices until revenue does.

WHAT NOT TO DO

Wait until you’re an engineer

You don’t need to master engineering best practices before you start using these systems.

The FDE's job: simple enough to explain to the owner, solid enough to maintain, monitor and fix.

Every tool is monitored while it runs

A failure alerts a person, not a log file

Every change is recorded and traceable

— When to automate

Every task earns its way to AI.

STEP 1

A person does it

Next when it repeats every week and the steps are written down.

STEP 2

Simple software does it

Next when the fixed steps run clean and only judgment calls are left.

STEP 3

AI drafts, a person approves

Next when it gets real cases as right as the person does.

STEP 4

AI runs it, watched

Only if a mistake is cheap and gets caught. Costly calls stay with a person.

80/20

Building the tool is 20% of the work. Getting the team to trust it is 80%.

— The honest part

What tools can't do on their own.

They don't fix a broken process

95% of AI projects show no measurable return. Usually the process underneath was never mapped. That work comes first.

Trust takes longer than the build

One bank: weeks to build, then months of pilots before people trusted it. Adoption came once it fit the steps they already used.

A tag in the code is not proof

A pixel on the site doesn't mean the conversion fires. Step zero confirms it first.
