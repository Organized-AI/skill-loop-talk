Now let’s follow one press of the Launch! button, like a meal in a restaurant: click, order, cook, serve. Stop one, click: the page you see is the frontend. It lives on this computer, called localhost, behind door 5173. Coders call those doors ports. Stop two, order: the page sends a request, GET /api/fact, which means “get me a fact.” It travels to door 8000, where the backend waits. The API is the menu: our backend knows exactly two orders, /api/health, which asks “are you awake?”, and /api/fact. Stop three, cook: the backend, written in Python, grabs one of our six facts at random, plus its source. It never makes facts up. We gave it these six, word for word, and checked them against NASA’s pages. Stop four, serve: the answer comes back as JSON, which works like a filled-in form: every answer has a label, here “fact” and “source.” The page reads the labels and shows the fact with a Source: NASA chip. The whole trip takes less than a second. Quick check: what happens if the server is asleep? (Answer: the page doesn’t crash. It says, “The fact server is asleep. Ask your mentor to wake it up.”)
